Readers from the future will find it hard to believe women could have clung so doggedly to a bizarre beauty standard designers and marketers manufactured out of whole cloth in the 20th century. It had no historical precedent, and was always easily debunked. Yet for generations even the most persuasive attempts to liberate women from this insanity fell on deaf ears. The explanations I've provided thus far aren't enough to fully account for such an extreme of stubbornness.

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/75/49/75490b33-7444-4a11-886d-0e9d3cca15d9/content/images/2025/11/vogue1920a.jpg)

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/75/49/75490b33-7444-4a11-886d-0e9d3cca15d9/content/images/2025/11/vogue1920b.jpg)

_This beauty ideal was never seen in history before the 1920s._

To fill the gap, some point to attainability. An hourglass figure, they claim, is a trait few are born with, whereas thinness is attainable for everyone, making it a more tolerable ideal. Yet this has never really been true, and it's even less true today. Nearly all women can improve their curves by tuning their weight correctly. And they can supplement nature not only with cosmetic surgery and exercise, but with good clothing choices too. So attainability doesn't differ enough to justify the level of intransigence.

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/75/49/75490b33-7444-4a11-886d-0e9d3cca15d9/content/images/2025/11/beige-crux-1.jpg)

_Self harm is not a reliable path to beauty._

An alternative explanation runs as follows. When they doubt their own value, women feel a compulsion to self harm, or at least to make a sacrifice of some sort. They then become addicted to a psychological loop of “I'm not good enough, therefore, I'll sacrifice something.” Because eating less amounts to a sacrifice, the thinner-is-better beauty ideal provides the perfect outlet for this compulsion, whether or not they actually succeed in getting skinny. And sacrifice feels virtuous as well.

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/75/49/75490b33-7444-4a11-886d-0e9d3cca15d9/content/images/2025/11/demibath1b.webp)

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/75/49/75490b33-7444-4a11-886d-0e9d3cca15d9/content/images/2025/11/demibath2b.webp)

_Desperate to feel pain? Why not try a cold shower?_

Pointless sacrifice is surprisingly sticky. Sacrifices of cattle, of humans, even of one's firstborn, “green” rituals that don't actually help the environment, and circumcision are among the historical examples. To break the cycle you have admit you've done harm for no good reason. But such an admission is too painful. So instead of accepting that a sacrifice is pointless and stopping, people double down and repeat it.

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/75/49/75490b33-7444-4a11-886d-0e9d3cca15d9/content/images/2025/11/camarlench-ignacio-pinazo-1849-1916-the-daughters-of-the-cid-1879.jpg)

_The links that fashion marketing forged between belief, status, self-worth, self-deprivation, and self-harm work together to create a potent psychological trap._

In sum, the last secret to the fashionable beauty standard's incredible hold on women is a psychological lock-in effect. It's similar to what we see in cults. And that's why its victims push away anyone who offers them freedom, while defending the very falsehoods that are hurting them.

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/75/49/75490b33-7444-4a11-886d-0e9d3cca15d9/content/images/2025/11/aig-brown-blinds.jpg)

Unfortunately, deprogramming the hardest hit is beyond my ability. The best I can do is to remind you that marketers and magazines and models promoting fashionable falsehoods are holding you back for their own gain. And, if you open your mind and give it a chance, the truth about feminine beauty will set you free.
