It's much easier to evaluate other people's beauty than our own. When we look at ourselves in the mirror, our minds start playing tricks on us, and it becomes very confusing. We may feel ugly or pretty depending on our mood rather than any real changes in our appearance. Due to the way cameras work, photos are distorted in other ways, and selfies in particular are often inaccurate representations. Furthermore, when we fixate on a particular feature we can imagine a problem where there isn't one.

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/75/49/75490b33-7444-4a11-886d-0e9d3cca15d9/content/images/2025/04/aig-rear-lace.jpg)

_She's worrying about her back fat. (Her back is not fat.)_

A common mistake is to misidentify your weak points. Women often worry about some minor or even nonexistent flaw while missing the bigger picture. Due to the psychological issues I just mentioned, it's hard to fix this on your own. But as a starting point, I suggest you look at the images of ideal women shown in this article and ask yourself if you're really trying to narrow the biggest gaps between you and them, or if you're instead obsessing over some relatively small and unimportant difference while ignoring a large and important difference.
