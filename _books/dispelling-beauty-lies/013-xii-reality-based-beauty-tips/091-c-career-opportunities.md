A popular myth is that women's talents, accomplishments, and careers have zero influence on men's affections. This myth is a product of overcorrection. It's true that these factors have less influence on female desirability than male desirability. It's also true that they have no impact on beauty. But their influence on men's choice of women is certainly not zero, and their influence on seduction isn't zero either.

_Hot for teacher._

Imaginary women in media produced for men are almost always portrayed as having traditionally feminine jobs. The most common are stewardess, maid, secretary, teacher, nurse, and [beautiful assistant](https://www.jsanilac.com/mala/). In contrast, very few are portrayed as having traditionally masculine jobs like CEO, soldier, or head doctor.

_Some jobs are definitely better than others, though it's unclear how much this impacts overall attractiveness._

As the poll above illustrates, what men find particularly unattractive are “bossy” jobs at the top of a hierarchy. These jobs are likely unattractive because they invert the natural dominant-submissive relationship of the sexes. So a job in corporate management is attractive in men but unattractive in women, just as a secretarial job is attractive in women but unattractive in men. Being promoted from secretary to management will reduce your attractiveness to some degree. If you already have a bossy job, it might be wise to downplay it outside of work.
