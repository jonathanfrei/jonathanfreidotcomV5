I've just pointed out the statistical problems with analyzing glamour models, and these apply even more strongly to celebrities, who are _less_ selected for beauty. In fact, some celebrities are given opportunities precisely because they're not “too” attractive. I'll explain the reasons for this later on.

In both traditional media and social media, popular discussions of beauty revolve around celebrities. For instance, who's the “it” girl of the season, and what are influencers saying about her? Or, who thinks this celebrity is hot, and who thinks that other celebrity is not? Nearly everyone is taken in by this sort of chatter at one time or another. The problem is, it's impossible to learn anything real and durable about beauty from it.

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/75/49/75490b33-7444-4a11-886d-0e9d3cca15d9/content/images/2025/08/marilyn-street.jpg)

_"Some celebrities are very beautiful” doesn't prove that celebrities or celebrity commentary in general are good sources of information about beauty._

Why so? Although celebrities do tend to be more attractive than average, they also rise to fame for reasons unrelated to their beauty. And the celebrities in vogue at any given moment are popular because of [trends](https://www.jsanilac.com/trust/#b-lets-paint-it-gray-trend-following) as much as beauty fundamentals. These trends come and go like the weather, and have little to do with genuine beauty. Sometimes outright ugly looks trend, and celebrities chase them anyway, like a stampede of herd animals running off a cliff together. In sum, any given season's popular celebs are a random draw from a bag that contains as much coal as silver, and hardly any gold.

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/75/49/75490b33-7444-4a11-886d-0e9d3cca15d9/content/images/2025/08/aig-buscrop-1.jpg)

_Beauty isn't about being famous._

As I mentioned earlier, the journalists and influencers who comment on celebrities are worse still. They're more concerned with creating a viral post, video, or news article than saying anything true. Some of them are quite happy to lie, and others will phrase random opinions as absolute truth just to get a reaction from the crowd. They're not thoughtful people, or usually even honest people. So the commentary on celebrities amounts to a _second_ random draw, this time from a bag that contains almost entirely coal.

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/75/49/75490b33-7444-4a11-886d-0e9d3cca15d9/content/images/2025/08/aig-demon-6.jpg)

_Actual demons: still more trustworthy than journalists and influencers._

When you add it all up, popular discussion of celebrities turns out to be coal stacked on top of coal. Even when a celebrity _is_ notably attractive, the commentary surrounding her includes so much noise and misinformation that everyone gets dumber just by listening to it.

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/75/49/75490b33-7444-4a11-886d-0e9d3cca15d9/content/images/2025/08/silly.jpg)

_Yes, influencers say dumb things about non-celebrity models too._

Imaginary women offer us a much better way to study feminine beauty. They have all the advantages I've previously listed in this section, and none of the problems with celebrity commentary I've just cited.

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/75/49/75490b33-7444-4a11-886d-0e9d3cca15d9/content/images/2025/08/aig-429.jpg)

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/75/49/75490b33-7444-4a11-886d-0e9d3cca15d9/content/images/2025/08/aig-elf-bath.jpg)

_Educational, even if "serious people" can't accept it._

So if you want to understand beauty, train yourself to ignore celebrity commentary completely. Just because everyone's talking about something doesn't mean they're saying anything true, or even anything not dumb. Imaginary women like AI girls and anime girls might seem silly, but they're still the clearest, most reliable, least confounded, and least biased evidence of what men really want.
