They may even fear an overtly attractive body will harm their reputation, whether or not their behavior is virtuous. This is a mistake. It's best to have _all_ the good things together.

Platonic beauty and hotness might be distinct. But they don't detract from each other. They complement and _enhance_ each other. “Feminine beauty,” as I discuss it in the rest of this article, comprises both.

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/75/49/75490b33-7444-4a11-886d-0e9d3cca15d9/content/images/2022/08/indian-genie-1.jpg)

Public discussion tends to emphasize platonic beauty over sex appeal because it sounds more respectable. Consequently, an honest account like the one you're now reading will appear to do the reverse.

_Big curves don't detract from beauty._

There are serious disadvantages to having a type of beauty that lacks sex appeal. You'll be put on a pedestal like an antique art object. Praised and even sought after, but not truly desired. You may even become a victim of infidelity. That's not a fulfilling state of affairs. It's in your own self-interest to pursue the full complement of feminine attractiveness.

By convincing you to handicap yourself, they reduce the chance you could poach their mates or potential mates. By denigrating attractive traits, they confuse and demoralize the naturally gifted.

“If you grow your hair long you'll be a slut.” “Oh yeah? You too.” “Well, that's settled then.” Women forcing each other to be less attractive is a big problem, and _that's_ the behavior that actually deserves shaming.

Even your friends might do this unconsciously. Know when to ignore them.
