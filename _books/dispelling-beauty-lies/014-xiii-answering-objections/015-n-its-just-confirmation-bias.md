_“You attract readers who already agree with you and then cite their opinions as proof of your views.”_

Anyone who believes that _Dispelling Beauty Lies_ can be refuted with this argument hasn't read it. The methods of analysis I developed for the first half of the article are completely immune to such criticism, because none of them have anything to do with my readers.

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/75/49/75490b33-7444-4a11-886d-0e9d3cca15d9/content/images/2025/07/book-1.jpg)

As far as the surveys cited in the second half of the article are concerned, it's reasonable to view them with some skepticism. Nevertheless, I'll explain in the appendix why it's unlikely that they were skewed by my readers to an important degree. Nor have I relied on these surveys in isolation. I've merely considered them along with a broader range of evidence to estimate which beautification and seduction techniques work best.
