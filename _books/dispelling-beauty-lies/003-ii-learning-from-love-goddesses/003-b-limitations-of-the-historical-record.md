So love goddesses and archetypes of beauty follow a pattern. However, they're not all identical. For instance, classical Greek statues don't have the small waist and wide hips evident in our other examples. Nor do the later Venuses copied from them. In fact, Van Gogh [quit art school](https://storage.ghost.io/c/75/49/75490b33-7444-4a11-886d-0e9d3cca15d9/content/images/2025/02/van-gogh.jpg?ref=jsanilac.com) when he was assigned to copy the _Venus de Milo_ because he believed its hips were too narrow.

_Because artists learn by imitating their predecessors, foundational models have a lasting influence. The classical Greek Venus (left) was the source for Roman copies (center) and Renaissance copies of copies (right, by Botticelli)._

While the persistence of this particular deviation was partly driven by a founder effect, its original motive is open to debate. Even in ancient times many influential artists were homosexual, perhaps including [Botticelli](https://storage.ghost.io/c/75/49/75490b33-7444-4a11-886d-0e9d3cca15d9/content/images/2023/10/botticelli.jpg?ref=jsanilac.com) himself.

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/75/49/75490b33-7444-4a11-886d-0e9d3cca15d9/content/images/2023/08/ancientgreekbeautystandards.png)

_Some scholars speculate that the ancient Greeks masculinized women to make them more palatable to the many victims of pederasty. An analogous phenomenon can be found in the world of modern fashion._

When we look earlier into Greek history, we can find the familiar figure seen in love-goddess representations around the world.

Nevertheless, exceptions are real and can't all be dismissed so easily.

_Two Roman Venuses. While the statuette at left follows the general pattern, the flat chests on frescoes from Pompeii are anomalous. I'll discuss this further in the next section._

Historical artwork may be compelling, but it's not enough to make a strong case. Furthermore, we can't rule out the possibility that current tastes differ from traditional ones. To reliably determine what men find attractive today, we'll have to examine more evidence.
