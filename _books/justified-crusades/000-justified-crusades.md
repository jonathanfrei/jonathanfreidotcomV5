## Part I – The Engine in Operation (c. 1095–1291/1453)

1. **The Invention of a Pan-Christian Project**  
    Shows how the call of 1095 created a new supra-political sacred enterprise, how that enterprise first proved itself in the multi-polity cooperation of the First Crusade, and how the same coordinating claim was repeatedly exercised across the twelfth and thirteenth centuries, producing tangible benefits for Latin Christian cohesion.
    
2. **Institutions of Coordination: Taxes, Orders, Logistics, and the Liturgy of the Cross**  
    Demonstrates the concrete mechanisms—fiscal, military-religious, logistical, and liturgical—that turned the idea of common action into a repeatable system across kingdoms.
    
3. **Capacity Accumulation: Finance, Shipping, Intelligence, and Knowledge Transfer**  
    Traces how the demands of sustained crusading generated cumulative gains in organizational and technical capacity that raised the collective baseline of Latin Christendom.
    
4. **The Sacred Identity: Martial-Missionary Confidence Across Kingdoms**  
    Establishes that the crusading system sustained a shared identity, visible in rhetoric, ritual, and self-conception, that supplied elites and participants with durable civilizational confidence.
    

## Part II – Dismantling the Engine (c. 1291–1571)

5. **The Progressive Abandonment: From Acre to the Last Major Expeditions**  
    Charts the stepwise erosion of the institutional will and machinery that had once made large-scale, coordinated crusading possible.
    
6. **Strategic Consequences: Frontiers, the Ottomans, and Resource Diversion**  
    Examines how the loss of coordinated eastern pressure contributed to frontier contraction and forced a costly defensive posture on parts of Christendom.
    
7. **Fragmentation Unleashed: National Churches, Confessional Rupture, and Internal War**  
    Argues that the removal of the external sacred coordinating project removed a key constraint on centrifugal forces, accelerating ecclesiastical and political fragmentation.
    

## Part III – Long-Term Costs and Partial Redirections

8. **The Multi-Generational Lag in Capacity and Confidence**  
    Shows domain-specific interruptions in the forms of coordinated capacity and collective confidence that the crusading system had previously institutionalized.
    
9. **Overseas Expansion as Incomplete Substitute**  
    Acknowledges the redirection of energy into Atlantic and global projects while demonstrating that the new enterprises did not fully replicate the old pan-Christian coordinating function or heal the internal fractures.
    
10. **Comparative Trajectories: Where External Sacred Projects Persisted**  
    Tests the thesis against cases in which expansionist religious-military or frontier pressures remained active, clarifying what difference the presence or absence of such a project made.
    

## Part IV – Synthesis

11. **The Integrated Mechanism Reassessed**  
    Weighs the strongest counter-arguments in their best form and returns a measured judgment on the three-fold engine and the costs of its dismantling.
    
12. **What the End of Crusading Reveals About Civilizational Cohesion**  
    Draws the larger implication: shared purpose, institutional capacity, and collective confidence are linked, and the deliberate or accidental dismantling of the institutions that sustain them carries long-term consequences.
