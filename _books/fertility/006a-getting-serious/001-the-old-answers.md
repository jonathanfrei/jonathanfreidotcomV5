Societies worried about continuity have not always waited for consent. Their stories and policies often move directly from the need for descendants to control over marriage, sex, and women's bodies. The record belongs in this book because it exposes the temptation inside demographic urgency, not because it supplies a usable playbook.
{: .lede}

## Cases to research

### The kidnapping of the Sabine women

- Separate the Roman foundation myth from recoverable history.
- Examine what the story assumes about women, marriage, conquest, assimilation, and civic continuity.
- Trace how later Romans used the episode to explain or legitimize the origins of a people.
- Ask why demographic necessity is presented as capable of transforming violence into founding legend.

### Colonial Paraguay

- Establish which period and regime the argument concerns.
- Research settlement patterns, marriage rules, relations among Spanish men, Indigenous women, the colonial state, and Jesuit missions.
- Distinguish voluntary unions, strategic incentives, legal hierarchy, exploitation, and retrospective mythmaking.
- Test claims about fertility and population growth against primary demographic evidence.

### Germany before and during the Second World War

- Separate Weimar-era population anxiety from Nazi family, marriage, abortion, sterilization, and racial policy.
- Examine marriage loans, honors for motherhood, restrictions on women's work, eugenics, and the division between births the regime encouraged and lives it marked for destruction.
- Show why a policy can raise or redirect births while corrupting the meaning of family and human equality.
- Avoid treating pronatalism as separable from the regime's racial hierarchy and coercive state.

## Analytical purpose

Each case should be tested against the same questions: Who was allowed to choose? Who bore the cost? Which lives counted? What did the state or story call a family? Did the apparent demographic result depend on violence, exclusion, conquest, or conditions that cannot be abstracted from it?
