A thought experiment becomes evasive if it considers only ideas its author already finds respectable. This section will map the full range of measures a frightened or ambitious society might propose. No proposal becomes prudent, permissible, or desirable merely by appearing on the map.
{: .lede}

## Categories to explore

### Radical redistribution toward families

- Large family allowances, debt cancellation, housing priority, pension credits, and tax systems built around dependents.
- A family-supporting income independent of uninterrupted paid employment.
- Public assumption of major costs now carried privately, including birth, education, healthcare, and care work.
- The distributional conflict between parents, nonparents, generations, and taxpayers.

### Reordering work, education, and adulthood

- Shorter credential paths and earlier economic independence.
- Career systems designed around unequal seasons of paid work and family care.
- National or civic service organized partly around family formation and community building.
- Restrictions or incentives affecting hours, mobility, remote work, and the geographic separation of kin.

### Cultural and institutional pressure

- Public honors, matchmaking institutions, marriage preparation, family-centered education, and media campaigns.
- Legal privileges attached to marriage and children.
- Communities that deliberately give parenthood greater status than autonomy or consumption.
- The line between forming aspiration and punishing dissent.

### Restrictions on practices that suppress births

- Laws and subsidies involving abortion, contraception, sterilization, divorce, and reproductive technology.
- Distinguish protection of human life and marriage from policies whose purpose is demographic compulsion.
- Examine enforcement, unintended effects, evasion, black markets, and unequal burdens.

### Direct demographic control

- Birth quotas, compulsory marriage, assigned partners, reproductive licensing, confinement, kidnapping, and sexual violence.
- Eugenic selection over who may or must reproduce.
- Treat these as boundary cases that reveal what demographic reasoning becomes when the person disappears.
- State plainly why some means remain impermissible regardless of projected benefit.

### Technological and utopian proposals

- Artificial gestation, in-vitro gametogenesis, embryo selection, cloning, and other attempts to industrialize reproduction.
- AI matchmaking, predictive fertility systems, and surveillance of intimate life.
- New settlements designed around family formation.
- The difference between healing infertility, assisting families, and manufacturing people to specification.

## Evaluation framework

For each measure, assess mechanism, likely magnitude, time horizon, reversibility, administrative power, distribution of costs, effects on marriage and children, compatibility with human dignity, and the kind of government or community capable of carrying it out.
