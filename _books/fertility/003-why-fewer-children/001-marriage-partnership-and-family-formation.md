Before there is a childcare problem or an IVF problem, there is usually a partnership problem. A child most often arrives after two people meet, decide to stay together, and build a household stable enough to welcome one. Each of those steps now happens later, and for more people, not at all.
{: .lede}

In the United States, the [median age at first marriage in 2025](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025/families-and-living-arrangements.html) was 30.8 for men and 28.4 for women, up from 23.5 and 21.1 in 1975. That change came with real gains: less pressure to marry young, more independence, and more room to leave a bad match. It also moved marriage deeper into the years when couples who want several children have less time to have them.

Marriage is also less common. Married couples made up 47 percent of American households in 2025, down from 66 percent fifty years earlier. Cohabitation has replaced part of the decline, and childbearing outside marriage has become common enough that marriage statistics alone do not describe family formation. Roughly half of first births reported in the [2015–2019 National Survey of Family Growth](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/122080) occurred before marriage; about half of those premarital births occurred within cohabiting unions.

The larger change is that durable partnership has become less predictable. Some cohabiting couples are effectively married in all but law. Others are still testing whether the relationship will last. A household that may dissolve next year is a harder place to plan a second or third child, even if a first child has already arrived.

## Why partnership begins later

A person can want marriage and children very much and still fail to find someone suitable who wants the same life at the same time.

The pool itself has changed. Women now earn more college degrees than men, while couples still tend to pair with someone of similar education. Steady employment matters too, particularly for men. A [national study of sexual activity](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7293001/) found that young men with lower income or part-time or no employment were more likely to report no sexual activity. Weak employment can make a man less confident about commitment and less attractive as a husband, while women have less economic reason than previous generations to accept an unreliable partner.

The expectations placed on a spouse have risen at the same time. Marriage is now asked to provide romance, emotional intimacy, shared purpose, and a fair division of domestic life for decades. Those are good things to want. They can also turn an ordinary human decision into a search for certainty that never quite arrives.

Dating apps widen the pool and sometimes create marriages that would not have happened otherwise. In 2022, [30 percent of American adults](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2023/02/02/from-looking-for-love-to-swiping-the-field-online-dating-in-the-u-s/) had used a dating site or app. But an apparently endless catalogue can keep choice provisional. The apps are good at presenting options. They cannot create trust, shared purpose, or the willingness to stop looking.

## Why the household stays fragile

Divorce does not explain the original postwar fertility decline by itself, but instability still affects family size. Separation ends a couple's childbearing years together and can consume years before another durable partnership forms. The effect falls especially hard on later births, which require enough confidence in the household to add another dependent person to it.

Single adulthood has also become a longer normal stage of life. Some people prefer it. Others drift into it through a sequence of sensible postponements while still assuming marriage will happen later. That difference can be hard to see at thirty and painfully obvious at forty.

I think marriage matters here for reasons larger than demography. It is a promise about permanence, and children benefit from knowing the adults who made them intend to remain. Public policy cannot manufacture that promise. A culture can still make commitment easier to reach and more honorable to keep.

Before the empty school or shrinking workforce comes the household that never formed.
