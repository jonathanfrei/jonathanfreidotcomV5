## Governing editorial decision

Chapter 3 should reject the search for one master cause without becoming an inventory. Its carrying pattern is accumulated delay and changed family formation: people meet later, commit later, face higher readiness thresholds, can postpone pregnancy reliably, and encounter biological limits before completing the families they imagined. Each section must add a distinct mechanism to that sequence.

### Inheritance from the revised Chapter 1

Chapter 1 removed its compressed causal survey so this chapter can establish the mechanisms rather than inherit conclusions. Recover and rework the material on marriage and partnership, reproductive timing, education and paid work, contraception, abortion, reproductive technology, culture, and biology within this chapter's accumulated-delay architecture.

- Treat the former Chapter 1 statements about contraception and abortion as claims to investigate, not reusable finished prose. Separate direct mechanism, estimated demographic magnitude, moral judgment, medical circumstance, and legal prescription.
- Develop marriage as a form of mutual obligation, embodied union, child-welcome, kinship, and formation rather than merely a variable correlated with births.
- Reuse the UN family-planning, WHO abortion, and UNFPA desired-family-size sources only as research leads after checking their exact measures, scope, and current versions.
- Do not restore Chapter 1's public-figure warning montage, policy preview, aging consequences, two-path model, or space chronology.
- Assume the reader already has Chapter 1's governing boundary: coercion is impermissible, voluntary choice is not the whole moral analysis, and no person is a demographic instrument. Add causal depth instead of repeating that boundary at length.

### Inheritance from the revised Chapter 2

The final Chapter 2 rewrite ends after establishing the demographic transition, regional variation, positive and negative momentum, and the conditional arithmetic of persistent below-replacement fertility. It asks why fertility kept falling after the population explosion was already over.

- Do not reteach crude birth rate, period TFR, completed cohort fertility, replacement fertility, population momentum, or projection uncertainty. Define a term briefly only when a direct-entry web reader needs it for a local claim.
- Inherit Chapter 2's finding that the classic transition does not explain why societies settle at very different fertility levels. Chapter 3 owns that missing causal account.
- Preserve the gains Chapter 2 names—survival, education, urban opportunity, reproductive agency, and longer lives—while examining the conflicts and mechanisms that accompany them.
- Use the final regional page's comparison carefully: high fertility under scarcity shows that absolute income is not a master explanation, but it does not invalidate housing, childcare, opportunity, or security constraints in affluent societies.
- Do not repeat Malthus, the Green Revolution, population-control history, regional TFR catalogs, or the 10- and 100-generation illustrations.
- Begin at the scale Chapter 2's final question opens: the life course through which meeting, commitment, readiness, postponement, conception, birth, and additional births accumulate into a national pattern.

### Final Chapter 1–2 reconciliation

- Chapter 1 has already supplied the evidence contract. Chapter 3 should make causal confidence visible in its verbs and source choices rather than pause to restate the taxonomy of observations, associations, causal claims, and judgments.
- Chapter 2 has already treated demographic transition, reproductive agency as a genuine gain, regional fertility differences, period-versus-cohort measures, momentum, and long-run arithmetic. Chapter 3 begins with the unresolved causal question, not another summary of decline.
- Chapter 2's regional evidence establishes that similar fertility outcomes can emerge from different institutions. Chapter 3 must therefore distinguish mechanisms that travel across countries from evidence confined to the United States or OECD.
- The final Chapter 2 sentence asks why societies moved below replacement and kept falling. Chapter 3 should answer through an accumulated life course, then hand Chapter 4 the smaller cohorts produced by that path.
- The current Chapter 4 opening begins with vacant Japanese homes and inherited infrastructure. Chapter 3 should end with births that did not occur, not preview aging, fiscal systems, schools, labor, or institutional contraction.

## Controlling question and provisional thesis

**Question:** Why do people in modern societies so often have fewer children, and why has no single explanation traveled well across countries?

**Thesis:** Low fertility emerges from a sequence of individually understandable changes that make partnership later, readiness more demanding, postponement more reliable, and recovery more biologically limited. Money matters, but it operates inside a larger social path into family life.
