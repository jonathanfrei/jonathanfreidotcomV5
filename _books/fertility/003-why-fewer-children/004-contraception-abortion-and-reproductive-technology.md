title: Contraception, abortion, and reproductive technology

Modern reproductive technology changed fertility in two opposite directions. Contraception and abortion made births easier to prevent. IVF and related treatments later made some otherwise impossible births possible. The asymmetry is the important part: postponing or preventing a birth is usually easier than creating one after age has reduced fertility.
{: .lede}

Reliable contraception separated sex from childbearing more completely than earlier generations could have imagined. In 2022–2023, [54.3 percent of American females ages fifteen to forty-nine](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/products/databriefs/db539.htm) were currently using contraception. Globally, [77.6 percent of women who wanted to avoid pregnancy](https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/report/2024/Goal-03/) had that need satisfied by a modern method in 2024.

That control has obvious benefits. Couples can avoid pregnancies they do not want and space children they do. It also changes the default path through adulthood. A couple can spend years in a sexual relationship while postponing parenthood with high confidence. Waiting no longer requires abstinence or much risk of an unintended child forcing the timetable forward.

This is how contraception connects to the rest of the chapter. It does not merely reduce unplanned births. It makes every other reason for delay easier to act on. The couple can wait for the wedding, the house, the promotion, or the feeling of readiness. A contraceptive routine can continue almost automatically while the decision to have a child requires a deliberate reversal.

## Abortion and births

Abortion affects fertility more directly because it ends an existing pregnancy before live birth. Around [73 million induced abortions](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/abortion) occur worldwide each year, according to the World Health Organization's estimate for 2015–2019. Guttmacher estimates [1,124,470 clinician-provided abortions](https://www.guttmacher.org/monthly-abortion-provision-study) in the United States in 2024.

Those numbers cannot simply be subtracted from births; some pregnancies would miscarry, and changes in law can affect sexual behavior, travel, and reporting. The underlying mechanism is still plain. Historical evidence from American states that legalized abortion before *Roe v. Wade* found an estimated [5 percent decline in births](https://www.nber.org/papers/w5615) relative to comparison states, with later work finding that much of the reduction persisted in completed fertility.

American law changed sharply with *Roe* in 1973 and again when *Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization* [overruled *Roe*](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/21pdf/19-1392_6j37.pdf) in 2022. The moral argument is larger than demography. Abortion also ends the life of an unborn child, which is precisely why demographic language can become morally antiseptic here. Still, for the narrower causal question of this chapter, legal access to abortion lowers births.

## Trying to recover time

Assisted reproductive technology works in the other direction. In 2022, U.S. clinics reported [435,426 ART cycles and 94,039 live-birth deliveries](https://www.cdc.gov/art/php/national-summary/index.html). For families who otherwise could not conceive, those births are real and extraordinary.

What treatment cannot reliably restore is the reproductive time that passed before treatment began. In CDC's 2021 national table, intended egg-retrieval cycles using a patient's own eggs led to live birth in 50.8 percent of cases under age thirty-five, 36.4 percent at thirty-five to thirty-seven, 23.4 percent at thirty-eight to forty, and 7.9 percent over forty. Those are cycle-level figures rather than guarantees for a patient, but the age pattern is hard to miss.

Egg freezing can preserve younger eggs for later use. IVF can overcome some causes of infertility. Future technologies may push the boundary further. None justifies telling young adults that biology has already become optional.

Modern medicine gave people an astonishing ability to say “not yet.” It is much less capable of making “now” succeed after enough years have passed.
