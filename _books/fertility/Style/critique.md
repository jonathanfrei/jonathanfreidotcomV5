# Critique pack

Run before marking a draft `review` or opening/updating a PR. Full trope examples: `ref/tropes.md`.

For each item: **pass**, **fail** (quote evidence), or **n/a**. Density matters—one careful use can be fine; clusters are not.

## Prose patterns

| ID | Check | Fail signals |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Short-fragment paragraphs | Most paragraphs 1–2 sentences without deliberate pacing |
| P2 | Negative parallelism stack | Repeated “It’s not X—it’s Y” / “Not X. Not Y. Just Z.” |
| P3 | Rhetorical Q→A | “The result? Devastating.” |
| P4 | Fake suspense | “Here’s the kicker/thing/deal” |
| P5 | Hand-holding | “Let’s delve/unpack/break this down,” “Think of it as…” |
| P6 | Inflated AI vocabulary | delve, tapestry, landscape (of X), leverage (verb), robust, harness |
| P7 | Serves-as dodge | “serves as,” “stands as,” “marks a pivotal moment” for plain “is” |
| P8 | Vague attributions | “experts say,” “observers note” without names |
| P9 | Invented labels | Multiple “X paradox/trap/creep” treated as established terms |
| P10 | Fractal summary | Restating what will be / was said every section |
| P11 | Signposted conclusion | “In conclusion,” “To sum up” |
| P12 | Em-dash addiction | Em dashes for drama throughout |
| P13 | Grandiose stakes | “will redefine / fundamentally reshape / next era” |
| P14 | Meta identity labels | “As a Catholic…,” “As a father…,” worldview credentialing |
| P15 | Generic opening | Dictionary def, “In today’s rapidly changing world” |
| P16 | Significance advertising | “interesting/important/useful/meaningful thing is…,” “This matters because…,” “That distinction is important,” or similar throat-clearing |
| P17 | Empty noun crutches | Clusters of something/thing/things |
| P18 | Parenthetical source dumping | Source links appended in parentheses when descriptive inline linking would read naturally |
| P19 | Example-announcing | Sentences that tell the reader an example is “useful,” “interesting,” or “revealing” instead of moving directly from evidence to the demonstrated point |
| P20 | Unnecessary elevation | Abstract/philosophical language where a person, action, or consequence would be clearer |
| P21 | Hedge-and-pivot repetition | Repeated “X, but Y,” “not X, but Y,” “although X, Y,” or similar qualify-then-advance constructions across nearby sentences or paragraphs |
| P22 | Paragraph-template repetition | Several consecutive paragraphs repeat the same topic sentence → evidence → caveat → synthesis architecture |
| P23 | Enumeration reflex | Repeated three-plus-item noun or example lists where fewer, better-chosen details would carry the point |
| P24 | Abstract historical summary | “Changed/reshaped the debate,” “gave new force,” “confounded claims,” or similar abstractions appear where concrete actors, events, numbers, policies, or consequences could do the work |
| P25 | Excessive rhetorical symmetry | Prose repeatedly resolves complications into neat balanced formulations instead of allowing unevenness, ambiguity, emphasis, or a paragraph to end without synthesis |
| P26 | False calm | The prose hedges or lowers the stakes where evidence supports urgency about compounding demographic or civilizational loss |
| P27 | Single-horizon thinking | A civilizational claim remains confined to the next budget cycle or forecast endpoint when a generational, centennial, or millennial view would materially change it |

## Voice preservation

Use `voice.md` as the authority for these checks. The purpose is not to make every piece casual; it is to prevent revision from polishing away the author's natural sound.

- **V1 Conversational sound:** Does the prose sound like a thoughtful person speaking to a familiar listener rather than prose composed to display writing skill?
- **V2 Natural speech retained:** Has revision unnecessarily removed contractions, mild qualifications, parenthetical asides, ordinary intensifiers, or casual vocabulary that fit the voice?
- **V3 Unhurried cadence:** Are sentences and paragraphs allowed to develop naturally, or has the draft been optimized for constant rhetorical impact, compressed cleverness, or punchy turns?
- **V4 Honest uncertainty:** Where the writer is genuinely uncertain or exploratory, does the prose preserve that epistemic texture rather than forcing categorical confidence?
- **V5 Earned ending:** Does the ending fit the piece's emotional temperature? Fail if it feels engineered into an aphorism, clever reversal, hard punchline, or grand resolution that the essay has not earned.
- **V6 Natural roughness:** Has editing made every paragraph equally tidy? Preserve occasional asymmetry, bluntness, and exploratory movement without weakening logic or accuracy.

A voice-preservation failure is not triggered by the mere absence of hedging, asides, intensifiers, or softness. These are available characteristics, not quotas. Judge whether the draft sounds consistent with `voice.md` and whether editing has made it less so.

## Humanization pass

For paragraphs heavy with philosophical, theological, economic, or technical abstraction:

1. Identify the human observation underneath.
2. Rewrite one level more concretely.
3. Restore specialized terms only where they add precision.
4. Preserve complexity; remove performance of complexity.

Test: **Would an intelligent person naturally say this over dinner?**

## Substance gates

- Clear purpose and meaningful thesis/takeaway?
- Each section earns its place; assumptions/counterarguments handled?
- Original vs generic summary; differentiated from existing content?
- Claims supportable; uncertainty honest; quotes in context?
- Audience level clear; examples relevant?
- When individual journeys are central, are they concrete enough to show change over time rather than merely list tools?
- Are primary/first-person accounts preferred for individual stories when available?
- Prose economical without polishing away authentic voice; ending adds value rather than restating the intro or manufacturing a flourish?
- Worldview applied when appropriate (`worldview.md`) **implicitly** rather than explained or credentialed?
- Does the argument reach a human consequence—what someone should admire, reject, choose, protect, or become—without a bolted-on “why it matters” section?
- If multiple examples support the thesis, is there a stronger carrying pattern (reversal, inheritance, symmetry, transformation, cause/effect) than simple accumulation?
- Is personal experience used as evidence, stakes, or payoff rather than authority? Is it placed where it has the most leverage?
- For cultural criticism: does the piece distinguish depiction from endorsement and examine what the work trains the audience to admire?
