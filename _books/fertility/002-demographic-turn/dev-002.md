## Governing editorial decision

Chapter 2 must earn the premise announced in Chapter 1. It should move from the old population-explosion frame through the demographic transition and into the uneven global fertility map, then finish by making momentum and compounding intuitive. It is the book's principal factual foundation, but it should read as a historical reversal rather than a demographic reference manual.

### Inheritance from the revised Chapter 1

The final August 27, 2026 Chapter 1 rewrite establishes only the evidence contract and minimum direct-entry context. Its renumbered `002-evidence.md` briefly distinguishes period and completed fertility, identifies replacement fertility as an approximate population benchmark, separates observations, associations, causal claims, projections, illustrations, interpretations, and moral judgments, and explains why near-term cohort inertia can coexist with distant uncertainty.

Chapter 2 therefore owns the full teaching sequence: exact definitions, demographic transition, age structure, positive and negative momentum, projection construction and variants, and compounding below replacement. Do not replay the Lambeth school case, the full stakes argument, Adams's Law, or the moral meaning of renewal. Begin with the historical reversal promised by Chapter 1's final question.

## Controlling question and provisional thesis

**Question:** How did the modern fear of too many people give way to a world of shrinking generations, and why is the turn easy to miss?

**Thesis:** The same demographic transition that produced the population explosion eventually reduced fertility across very different societies. Mortality fell first, births followed, and population momentum concealed the reversal; once fertility remains below replacement, momentum and compounding eventually work in the opposite direction.

Do not paste the thesis mechanically into the chapter.
