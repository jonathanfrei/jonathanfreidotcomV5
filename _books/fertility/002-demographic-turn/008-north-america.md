deck: Immigration and inherited age structure can keep populations growing below replacement

The United States and Canada still grow. Mexico remains younger than either. North America is a useful reminder that a rising population does not mean one generation is replacing itself.
{: .lede}

The United States has remained below replacement since 2007. Final federal data recorded 3.63 million births in 2024. The general fertility rate fell slightly even as the number of births rose, because counts and rates use different denominators. The total fertility rate remained around 1.6 births per woman.

Canada has moved further. Its TFR reached 1.25 in 2024, entering the range the United Nations describes as ultra-low. Yet Canada had recently experienced rapid population growth because immigration added large numbers of people, many of them young adults. A national population can become larger while the family pattern beneath it moves further below replacement.

Mexico completes the continental reversal. Its 2023 demographic survey estimated TFR at 1.6, down from 2.07 in the comparable 2018 survey. Mexico retains a younger age structure, so births can remain numerous and population can grow after the period rate falls below replacement.

The old gradient—low fertility in Canada, somewhat larger families in the United States, and much larger families in Mexico—has largely disappeared. The three countries differ less in whether fertility is below replacement than in the age structures they inherited and the scale and direction of migration across their borders.

## What immigration changes

Immigration changes national demography quickly because migrants are often young adults. They can fill jobs, form families, care for relatives, and become part of the institutions of the receiving country. The United Nations projects immigration to become the main driver of population growth in the United States, Canada, and dozens of other countries by the middle of the century.

That contribution is real. Calling immigration a transfer should not make the people moving sound like entries shifted between ledgers. They leave one home and build another. Their departure also changes the communities and families they leave, and no national migration policy adds a person to the world total.

Migrant fertility does not remain fixed either. The OECD finds broad convergence toward host-country patterns across generations, with meaningful variation by origin, destination, age at arrival, and migration channel. A country can use immigration to adapt to low fertility for a long time. It cannot assume a permanent supply of young adults unaffected by the same global transition.

North America can therefore postpone visible contraction longer than places with older populations and less migration. That is an advantage, not an illusion. It is also why the demographic turn can remain hidden beneath a rising national head count.
