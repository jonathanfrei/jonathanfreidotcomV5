## Research notes

Figures below are for drafting, not for pasting un-checked. Verify vintage, geography, and definition (TFR vs completed fertility vs desired fertility) at prose time.

### Sabines

- Livy, *Ab Urbe Condita* 1.9–13. English: Canon Roberts translation, [Perseus / ToposText](https://topostext.org/work/142); also [Gutenberg, Books 1–8](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/19725/19725-h/19725-h.htm).
- Plot to use: new Rome is short of women; neighbors refuse *conubium*; Consualia; seizure of maidens; parents denounce violation of hospitality; Romulus says parental pride caused it and offers civic standing; later the women throw themselves between armies; fusion of peoples.
- Livy presents the motive as population, not lust, and claims no sexual assault during the abduction. That is Livy's framing. The first act is still seizure. English "Rape of the Sabine Women" is *raptus* (seizure).
- Plutarch, *Life of Romulus*, as a second ancient telling.
- Treat as foundation myth, not eighth-century reportage.

### Nazi natalism

- Marriage loans (*Ehestandsdarlehen*): Law for the Reduction of Unemployment, 1 June 1933. Interest-free loan up to 1,000 RM in household vouchers; originally the wife left paid work; 25% forgiven per live child (*abkindern*); restricted by "Aryan" descent, hereditary health, political reliability. Overview: [Wikipedia, "Marriage loan"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage_loan), with the 1933 statute as the primary to cite in prose.
- Honour Cross of the German Mother, decreed 1938, first awarded 21 May 1939 (Mother's Day): bronze / silver / gold for four, six, eight children. No cash. Hitler Youth ordered to salute. Earlier Honour Cards for mothers of three asked shops to give preferential treatment (queue-jumping). Lisa Pine's work on Nazi family policy is the standard secondary.
- Law for the Prevention of Hereditarily Diseased Offspring, 14 July 1933 (RGBl. I S. 529): compulsory sterilization; on the order of 300,000–400,000 people. Abortion policy split: restricted for the "valuable," opened for those marked for elimination.
- Empirical caution: period births rose from Depression lows in the mid-1930s; some of that is tempo/marriage catch-up; war then collapsed the rate. Do not claim the regime produced a high-fertility people. Do claim it selected *who* may reproduce.

### Israel

- National TFR near 3 is an OECD outlier and is not one number.
- Alex Weinreb / Taub Center, using CBS: Haredi on the order of 6–6.5; dati (religious) near 4; traditional above replacement; secular Jewish near 2.0. See [Sapir, "Faith & Fertility in Israel"](https://sapirjournal.org/faith/2024/faith-and-fertility-in-israel/) and [JPPI 2023](https://jppi.org.il/en/%D7%94%D7%A2%D7%A8%D7%9B%D7%94-%D7%A9%D7%A0%D7%AA%D7%99%D7%AA-2023/s/5.4/).
- Arab/Muslim TFR has fallen sharply over decades (from very high toward ~3 or below, depending on vintage and group).
- Recent reporting (2026) that Haredi TFR has eased from its peak. Verify CBS before locking a number.
- Secular Israeli Jewish fertility near 2.0 is still high relative to secular populations elsewhere. That is part of why "just copy the child allowance" fails: even the low-religiosity slice is not Korea.

### Amish, LDS, other subcultures

- Lyman Stone, ["Amish fertility in the United States"](https://www.demographic-research.org/articles/volume/52/26), *Demographic Research* 52, art. 26 (2025): TFR just over 6 from ACS and settlement registries; ASFR peaked at 20–29; very low non-marital fertility.
- Retention is high in many affiliations and not uniform. Do not treat "the Amish" as one TFR.
- LDS: still above the U.S. mean, but the extra-child advantage has narrowed. Utah TFR was 1.85 in 2022, no longer the old outlier. NPR (31 Oct 2025) on church leadership noticing the decline; Stephen Cranney on falling share of LDS women 18–45 with a child at home (70% in 2008 → 59% in 2022).
- Do not treat "Catholic" as high-fertility. Use only high-practice pockets with clean data.

### Status, seeing, propaganda

  - Each extra child born to an admired public figure associated with up to ~0.15 more desired children; larger for women; comparable to the sibling effect. Stone's Daily Wire write-up: ["America Needs More Famous Families"](https://ifstudies.org/in-the-news/america-needs-more-famous-families).

### Russia

- 12 November 2024: State Duma approved a ban on "propaganda in favor of a childfree lifestyle." [FIDH summary](https://www.fidh.org/en/issues/lgbtqi-rights/russia-ban-on-propaganda-in-favor-of-childfree-lifestyles). Cite the statute in prose, not only FIDH.
- March 2026 press (Straits Times, Kyiv Post, and others) reported Health Ministry guidelines to refer women who do not want children to psychologists. **Primary ministry text was not located in this research pass.** Do not treat the viral headline as the regulation until the guideline is in hand. Lisa Britton's post (21 Mar 2026) is how the item entered the source pile, not a source.

### Singapore 2026

- Primary: [Marriage & Parenthood measures, National Day Rally 2026](https://www.population.gov.sg/marriage-parenthood-measures-at-national-day-rally-2026/) and [Made For Families, NDR 2026](https://www.madeforfamilies.gov.sg/ndr-2026-supporting-families).
- SG Child Support Package replaces Baby Bonus and Large Families Scheme: up to about S$62,000 per citizen child in the new package, ~S$70,000 including MediSave/Edusave already on the books, from birth to 17, **same baseline regardless of birth order**.
- Pieces: S$10,000 Baby Gift in year one; S$2,000 a year ages 1–16; CDA First Step; PSEA top-up at 17.
- Childcare leave: 8 / 10 / 12 days by number of children, through age 12; government reimbursement of statutory leave up to a cap.
- Housing: extra BTO ballot chances per child; income ceiling raised.
- Childcare fee-cap path toward much cheaper full-day care (the X Freeze recap said S$150/S$300 by 2030; confirm against the government pages before using those numbers).
- This is the latest respectable stack. Use it to show that "make it easier" is still being tried at large scale. Do not rebuild chapter 5.

### Pensions that already credit children

- Germany: *Kindererziehungszeiten* — pension points for years of childrearing (typically three years per child in the current system). Existence proof for "retool social security," not a fertility miracle.
- France: insurance quarters credited at birth and on subsequent birthdays, up to a cap per child (d'Addio / OECD childcare-credit literature).
- These are cousins of a childless tax: they change the lifetime bargain without a line item labeled "punishment."

### Female education and the fertility window

- Two mechanisms, do not collapse them:
  1. **Incarceration effect of school:** time in school delays marriage and first birth.
  2. **Preference and opportunity cost after graduation:** higher earnings, later marriage-market entry, smaller desired families, more effective contraception.
- Starting points: World Bank blogs on female education and childbearing; IZA *Female education and its impact on fertility*; Kleven child-penalty work already in chapter 3.
- TradAdvocate's university claim is about delay, debt, *and* worldview. The third is cultural; do not pretend SAT scores are the whole story.

### Historical bachelor taxes and status rituals (catalog color)

- Rome: *aes uxorium*, a tax on unmarried men, attributed to the censors. Use lightly; sources are thin.
- Fascist Italy, 1926–1927: bachelor tax under Mussolini.
- Soviet Union, 1941: tax on bachelors, single women, and small families.
- Point: taxing the unmarried or childless is not a 2026 invention. Magnitude and enforcement vary; humiliation is often the point.
