title: Population as the foundation of expansion
deck: Spaceflight concentrates rare skill, while settlement requires the ordinary depth of a whole people
eyebrow: More than a crew
---

Twelve people walked on the Moon during Apollo. Behind them stood about 400,000 workers. The astronauts were the visible tip of a society large enough to train specialists while the rest of the country kept running.
{: .lede}

NASA's [account of the Apollo workforce](https://www.nasa.gov/specials/apollo50th/wehackthemoon.html) includes people who calculated trajectories and packed food. That range is easy to miss in the photographs. A rocket depends on metallurgy and software, then on a payroll system that pays the metallurgist. The mission draws from universities it did not build and families that raised the workers long before NASA hired them.

A larger population does not automatically create more capable people. Education and freedom determine how much talent can emerge. Population supplies the persons among whom rare combinations of ability and interest can appear. It also supports the division of labor that lets an excellent propulsion engineer spend years on propulsion because somebody else knows how to keep the water clean.

## The relay between generations

Space development is already multigenerational. Engineers who began on Apollo trained people who worked on the shuttle and the International Space Station. Some knowledge moved through drawings; much of it traveled through a senior worker standing beside a younger one when a test failed. The vehicle changes, while the need for succession remains.

This is where an aging, shrinking population creates a particular weakness. A short project can recruit heavily from one cohort. A century of building cannot. It needs young workers arriving as older experts retire, and enough depth that the space program does not strip power plants of the same scarce technical labor. The demographic problem reaches far beyond finding a few brilliant scientists. They need a wide base beneath them.

Governments can respond through better training and machines that extend each worker. Immigration also moves an engineer where her skill is more useful, though it does not enlarge the global generation. Better education finds ability that older institutions overlooked, which may be the largest available gain. Eventually the number of possible apprentices still counts.

## A settlement needs ordinary people

Exploration selects narrow crews because every seat is costly. A settlement gradually includes people whose main gifts would never earn a place on a launch manifest. Someone will open a bakery because people want bread that tastes good. Someone else will teach a difficult child patiently. These lives can look inefficient from mission control. They are evidence that the outpost is becoming a home.

Population scale also permits disagreement. A crew of six can operate under a commander and a detailed flight plan. A city contains competing plans and loyalties. That variety is inconvenient. It is also how a community stops depending on a single institution's judgment.

The population needed for expansion is a growing field of free people from whom skill and unexpected vocation arise. Spaceflight will use a tiny share of them. Their depth gives the tiny share somewhere to stand.
