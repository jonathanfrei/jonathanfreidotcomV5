A baby born after a turn in fertility changes the birth registry immediately but, for years, almost nothing in a pension account or staffing roster. Demographic recovery begins as a household reality and then spends decades becoming public capacity.

**The inherited curve** Births per woman, world average, UN estimates through 2023. More than half of countries are already below replacement even while the global average remains near two. *[Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/children-per-woman)*

That delay is the first reality any serious response to fertility decline has to accept. In 2024, more than half of the world's countries and areas were already below replacement fertility, and nearly one fifth were below 1.4 births per woman. The women who will have most of the children of the 2030s have been born, as have the workers who will support the oldest populations during those years. Policy can change what happens among them. It cannot add a missing cohort of thirty-year-olds.

The age structure of the next few decades is therefore substantially inherited. A smaller entering class will meet a larger retiring class while hospitals and care homes need more labor, employers compete for the same reduced pool of workers, and public budgets carry promises made under a different demographic balance. Those imbalances cannot be fixed quickly without pretending that people are interchangeable units who can simply be moved around the world.

Immigration can give a particular country younger workers and needed neighbors, and it remains useful and often humane. It also draws from a world in which fertility is falling across most regions, while immigrants commonly move toward the fertility pattern of their destination. Migration can postpone some national shortages. It cannot create a global successor generation.

None of this makes the longer future fixed. A child born after a fertility recovery will eventually become an adult in a society with one more person than it otherwise would have had; she may become a parent, engineer, caregiver, teacher, artist, or something nobody planning today's pension system can anticipate. Her life is not valuable because of any of those possible uses. The demographic point is simpler: another person also enlarges the field of relationships, work, care, and invention available to everyone else.

That is the difference between lag and destiny. It prevents next year's birth rate from being sold as an instant fiscal repair while also preventing the slow pace of repair from becoming an excuse for surrender. We cannot choose the population of 2040 from scratch. We are still choosing the population that comes after it.
