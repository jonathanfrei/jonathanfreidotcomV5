# Research: The surviving exceptions

## What this section has to establish

Packages far from the low-fertility mainstream can produce large, durable fertility. Not a recommended model. Evidence of magnitude. Israel must be disaggregated. Amish TFR just over 6 is the cleanest rich-world subculture number. LDS advantage has narrowed — say so.

## Suggested order of evidence

National Israel TFR (~2.9) is an OECD outlier → CBS/Taub religiosity split (Haredi ~6.5, dati ~4, secular Jewish ~2.0) → Arab decline → Amish (Stone 2025) → LDS narrowed advantage → ecology (early marriage, schools, matchmaking, costly exit) → state cannot photocopy the child allowance.

## Source cards

### 1 — CBS: fertility by religiosity (primary)

- **Use for:** Israel is not one number.
- **Kind:** Primary.
- **Cite:** Israel Central Bureau of Statistics, *Fertility of Jewish and Other Women in Israel, by Level of Religiosity, 1979–2022* (publication updated through 2024 on the CBS site).
- **Link:** https://www.cbs.gov.il/en/publications/Pages/2023/Fertility-of-Jewish-and-Other-Women-in-Israel-by-Level-of-Religiosity-1979%E2%80%932022.aspx
- **Claim:** Official series by religiosity exists. Use the latest table in the PDF at draft time rather than a journalist’s round number.
- **Caveat:** The HTML landing page is thin; download the publication.

### 2 — JPPI / Weinreb compilation of CBS

- **Use for:** A readable split if the CBS PDF is awkward in prose.
- **Kind:** Secondary, based on CBS.
- **Cite:** “Fertility Rates by Religion and Religiosity in Israel,” in *The Situation and Dynamics of the Jewish People in 2023*, JPPI.
- **Link:** https://jppi.org.il/en/%D7%94%D7%A2%D7%A8%D7%9B%D7%94-%D7%A9%D7%A0%D7%AA%D7%99%D7%AA-2023/s/5.4/
- **Numbers (JPPI, citing CBS, vintage ~2020s):**
  - Haredi: fluctuated 6.0–7.3 over 40 years; **6.45** in the vintage they report.
  - Dati (religious): 3.7–4.3; **3.88**.
  - Traditional-religious: **2.82**.
  - Traditional-not-so-religious: **2.34**.
  - Secular Jewish: 1.8–2.3 over 40 years; **2.00**.
  - Arab Christians 4.7 → 2.1; Druze 7.3 → 2.3; Muslims 9.2 → 3.3 since the 1960s.
- **Claim:** Even secular Israeli Jewish TFR ~2.0 exceeds almost the entire OECD (JPPI notes Mexico as the comparison). That is why “copy the child allowance” fails: the low-religiosity slice is still not Korea.
- **Caveat:** Composition: Haredi weight in the population is rising, which props up the national average even if subgroup rates ease.

### 3 — IDI Haredi statistical report 2024

- **Use for:** Haredi decline from a peak, early marriage, geography.
- **Kind:** Secondary, CBS-based.
- **Cite:** Israel Democracy Institute, *Statistical Report on Ultra-Orthodox Society in Israel 2024*.
- **Link:** https://en.idi.org.il/haredi/2025/?chapter=63076
- **Numbers:** Haredi TFR 7.5 (2003–2005) → **6.5 (2021–2023)**. Still far above other Jewish women (~2.2 in that comparison). Mean age at first marriage ~22 women / ~23 men (2023); first birth ~23.5 / 24.5. Population ~1.45 million in 2025, 14.3% of Israel; ~4.2% annual growth. 57% under age 20.
- **Claim:** Extremely high fertility can ease and remain extreme. Family limitation exists inside the community (Sapir/Okun: not “natural fertility” at 10).

### 4 — Taub / Weinreb 2025 demography

- **Use for:** Jewish TFR 3.07 in 2024; Muslim 2.7 and falling.
- **Kind:** Secondary.
- **Cite:** Alex Weinreb, Taub Center demography update 2025 (PDF circulating as `Demography-2025-ENG`).
- **Link (discussion):** https://blogs.timesofisrael.com/demographic-reversal-israel-chooses-life/
- **Claim:** Jewish fertility now exceeds Arab fertility. Split by religiosity still runs Haredi >6, religious ~4, traditional ~3, secular ~2.
- **Caveat:** Blog is not the table. Prefer Taub PDF at draft time.

### 5 — Sapir: faith and fertility in Israel

- **Use for:** Secular Israeli completed families often three; Haredi ~6.5 is chosen, not maximal.
- **Kind:** Secondary essay by a demographer.
- **Cite:** “Faith & Fertility in Israel,” *Sapir*, 12 Aug 2024.
- **Link:** https://sapirjournal.org/faith/2024/faith-and-fertility-in-israel/
- **Claim:** National TFR held around three for decades. Haredi ~6.5. Self-described secular Jews “most commonly have three children by the time they complete their families,” higher than Diaspora counterparts. Haredi fertility is below physiological maximum and involves family limitation.

### 6 — Amish TFR (best rich-world subculture number)

- **Use for:** A high-fertility population inside the United States that can be measured two ways.
- **Kind:** Primary research article.
- **Cite:** Lyman Stone, Cory Anderson, and Stephanie Thiehoff, “Amish fertility in the United States: Comparative evidence from the American Community Survey and Amish population registries,” *Demographic Research* 52, art. 26 (29 Apr 2025): 869–886.
- **Link:** https://www.demographic-research.org/articles/volume/52/26
- **DOI:** 10.4054/DemRes.2025.52.26
- **PDF:** https://www.demographic-research.org/volumes/vol52/26/52-26.pdf
- **Claim:** ACS identification (Pennsylvania Dutch, no telephone, farming combinations) and CAPED-2010s registries **agree**: TFR **just over six**; ASFR highest ages 20–29; non-marital fertility very low.
- **Caveat:** Identification in ACS is probabilistic. Retention and TFR vary by affiliation; do not write “the Amish” as one people with TFR 10. Neighboring *Demographic Research* 53(25) (Anderson et al.) on Holmes County neighbors’ attitudes predicting live births is optional color.

### 7 — U.S. Haredi in ACS

- **Use for:** High fertility is not only an Israeli security story.
- **Kind:** Primary research.
- **Cite:** Lyman Stone et al., “Ultra-Orthodox fertility and marriage in the United States: Evidence from the American Community Survey,” *Demographic Research* 49, art. 29.
- **Link:** https://www.demographic-research.org/articles/volume/49/29
- **Claim:** U.S. Haredi marriage and fertility are also extreme in ACS. Use if the “expensive city, no baby bonus” claim needs a U.S. exhibit (Molson).

### 8 — LDS: narrowed advantage

- **Use for:** Honesty. A high-fertility religious culture can converge.
- **Kind:** Secondary + church primary.
- **Cite:**
  - NPR, “Latter-day Saints are having fewer children,” 31 Oct 2025. https://www.npr.org/2025/10/31/nx-s1-5535654/latter-day-saints-are-having-fewer-children-church-officials-are-taking-note
  - Stephen Cranney, analysis of women 18–45 with a child at home: **70% (2008) → 59% (2022)**.
  - Utah TFR **1.85 in 2022**, near the national mean (geocurrents / NVSS).
  - Dallin H. Oaks, General Conference Oct 2025, noting LDS birth rates remain higher than national but have declined “significantly.”
- **Claim:** Still an advantage, no longer 1981 (TFR ~3.3 vs U.S. ~1.8). Do not treat Utah as the Amish.

### 9 — Pew religion and fertility (already in chapter 3)

- **Use for:** U.S. Christians 2.2 vs unaffiliated 1.8 at ages 40–59, if a small domestic comparison is needed.
- **Cite:** Gregory Smith et al., “Religion, fertility and child-rearing,” Pew, 2025.
- **Already in the book?** Chapter 3, culture section. Cite, don’t rebuild.

## Ecology checklist (not a source — a drafting list)

Early marriage, high expected parity, matchmaking, own schools, geographic concentration, mutual aid, status inside the group, a household a father can support, costly exit. You cannot photocopy the child allowance and leave the rest. Selection and people who leave belong on the page.
