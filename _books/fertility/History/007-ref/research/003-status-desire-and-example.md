# Research: Status, desire, and example

## What this section has to establish

Desire is socially contagious. A culture already runs a PR campaign. Status is ordinal — raising parenthood can mean lowering other lives. Shame, honors, and pathologizing childlessness are tools with history. Description first; sorting waits for permission.

## Suggested order of evidence

IFS 2026: helpful friends (~+1 desired child) and celebrity parity (~+0.15) → catch: desires more than intentions → Mandel/visible pregnancy as image → Chesterton 1927 (correct source) → Russia childfree-propaganda law → psychologist-referral as press, not yet primary → Nazi Honour Cards / Mother’s Cross as historical cousin (point back to old answers).

## Source cards

### 1 — IFS 2026 State of Fertility (load-bearing)

- **Use for:** Peer and celebrity culture as fertility policy.
- **Kind:** Primary research report.
- **Cite:** Lyman Stone and Peter Foreshaw Brookes, *The Demographic Dead End: 2026 State of Fertility Report*, Institute for Family Studies, July 2026.
- **Link:** https://ifstudies.org/report-brief/the-demographic-dead-end-2026-state-of-fertility-report
- **Survey:** 4,700+ Americans ages 18–50.
- **Numbers to use:**
  - U.S. TFR below **1.6**; Americans aspire to about **2.4**; think they will have about **2**.
  - Under-30s, bottom 10% least-helpful friends: desired family size ~**1.7**; top 10% most helpful: ~**2.8**. Among couples who want more children, unhelpful friends ~20–30% intend another vs ~40–50% with the most helpful friends (~10 percentage points).
  - Average parity of close ties has **twice** the association of childhood siblings with desired family size.
  - Each extra child born to the most-admired public figure: up to **+0.15** desired children; larger and more credible for women; similar in size to the sibling effect.
- **Catch (use it):** Number of peers with kids and celebrity family size shape **aspirations**. They do **not** strongly predict **intentions** among people whose ideals already exceed current parity. Intentions need friends who show up. “Demonstrative exposure shapes aspirations… behavioral intentions require more than just a pro-family public culture. They require peers who show up to change diapers.”
- **Also in the report (catalog, not this section):** American Birthday Accounts; care credits; the line that paying Taylor Swift a billion dollars to have children is empirically possible as a fertility intervention. That sentence belongs in the catalog or permission, attributed, as a thought experiment the authors themselves “would not endorse.”

### 4 — Chesterton, 1927 — correct source

- **Use for:** Serving an employer vs helping a husband. Also useful in the work section; do not quote at length twice.
- **Kind:** Primary.
- **Cite:** G. K. Chesterton, “Social Reform versus Birth Control,” 1927. **Not** *What’s Wrong with the World*. Memes often insert “[feminism]” and mis-attribute.
- **Link:** https://www.gkc.org.uk/gkc/books/Social_Reform_B.C.html
- **Quote (exact):** “it is mixed up with a muddled idea that women are free when they serve their employers but slaves when they help their husbands; it is ignorant of the very existence of real households where prudence comes by free-will and agreement.”
- **Context:** The “it” is birth-control ideology as Chesterton sees it, in an essay attacking Malthusianism as a capitalist argument against a family wage. Nearby: he cannot understand how wage-work under a richer stranger is “a freer condition than that of companionship with the man she has herself freely accepted.”
- **Caveat:** Quote accurately. Do not let a meme write the sentence.
