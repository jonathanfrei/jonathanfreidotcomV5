# Research: The old answers

## What this section has to establish

Historical extremes get the same rubric as a baby bonus: mechanism, who may reproduce, who pays, what is destroyed along with the births. Two cases: Sabine legend (finish what the intro opened) and Nazi natalism.

## Suggested order of evidence

Livy 1.13 (women stop the war; two peoples become one; population doubled) → Plutarch 14 (numbers, Hersilia, “not wantonness”) → Nazi marriage loan as statute (RGBl 1933 I 323) → Mother’s Cross → sterilization law → period births rose, completed fertility did not become a high-fertility people.

## Source cards

### 1 — Livy 1.13 — the women make a people

- **Use for:** How the myth transfigures seizure into founding.
- **Kind:** Primary.
- **Cite:** Livy 1.13. Roberts translation.
- **Link:** https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.02.0151:book=1:chapter=13
- **Quote:** The Sabine women “dared to go amongst the flying missiles… beseeching their fathers on this side, on that their husbands, that fathers-in-law and sons-in-law should not stain themselves with impious bloodshed.” Then: “not only did they agree on peace, but they made one people out of the two.” “In this way the population was doubled.”
- **Claim:** Livy’s landing is fusion and doubled population. The women’s intervention is the moral alibi the legend needs.
- **Caveat:** Myth. Thirty *curiae* named after the women; Livy says there were more women than names.

### 2 — Livy on *raptus*

- **Use for:** Vocabulary, one sentence.
- **Kind:** Primary. Latin: *penuria mulierum*; *ad rapiendas virgines discurrit* (1.9.10).
- **Link (Latin):** https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.02.0026:book=1:chapter=9 (English Roberts; Latin Conway/Walters available on Perseus as 1999.02.0160).
- **Claim:** The English title is later. The Latin act is seizure of maidens at a festival.

### 3 — Plutarch, *Life of Romulus* 14

- **Use for:** A second ancient telling; numbers; the “not wantonness” defense.
- **Kind:** Primary.
- **Cite:** Plutarch, *Life of Romulus* 14. Bernadotte Perrin, Loeb.
- **Link:** https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Plutarch/Lives/Romulus*.html
- **Claim:** Fourth month after founding, at the Consualia. Some said thirty maidens (and that Romulus wanted war more than wives); Valerius Antias 527; Juba 683; all maidens except Hersilia taken by mistake. “The strongest defence which Romulus could make” is that they took only one married woman, “since they did not commit the rape out of wantonness, nor even with a desire to do mischief, but with the fixed purpose of uniting and blending the two peoples.”
- **Caveat:** Plutarch is compiling variants. Use for the *apology structure*, not as a headcount.

### 4 — Nazi marriage loans — the statute

- **Use for:** A pronatalist instrument that is also a labor-market and racial filter.
- **Kind:** Primary.
- **Cite:** Gesetz zur Verminderung der Arbeitslosigkeit, 1 June 1933, Abschnitt V, RGBl. I S. 323.
- **Link (scan):** http://alex.onb.ac.at/cgi-content/alex?aid=dra&datum=1933&page=448&size=45
- **Also:** https://www.servat.unibe.ch/dns/RGBl_1933_I_323_G_Arbeitslosigkeit.pdf
- **Claim:** Interest-free *Ehestandsdarlehen* up to 1,000 RM in household vouchers for German nationals marrying. Original condition: the future wife had been employed and left work. Later, one quarter forgiven per live child (*abkindern*). Eligibility screened for “Aryan” descent, hereditary health, political reliability.
- **Secondary for numbers:** Wikipedia “Marriage loan” and de.wikipedia “Ehestandsdarlehen” collect the 1.1 million loans by end-1938 figure; prefer a monograph (Lisa Pine, or the IÉSEG 2023 paper on census 1933/1939/1970) when quoting magnitudes.
- **Caveat:** Dual purpose: get women out of jobs for unemployed men *and* raise approved births. Do not separate “family policy” from the racial state.

### 5 — Honour Cross of the German Mother

- **Use for:** Status as policy; queue-jumping as a cousin of Molson’s airport lines.
- **Kind:** Primary decree + secondary description.
- **Cite:** Stiftungsverordnung über die Stiftung des Ehrenkreuzes der Deutschen Mutter, 16 December 1938; first awards 21 May 1939 (Mother’s Day).
- **Claim:** Bronze / silver / gold for four, six, eight children. No cash. Hitler Youth ordered to salute wearers. Earlier Honour Cards for mothers of three asked shops and offices to give preferential treatment.
- **Secondary:** Lisa Pine, *Nazi Family Policy, 1933–1945*; Jill Stephenson, *Women in Nazi Society*.
- **Caveat:** French *Médaille de la Famille française* (1920) is a non-Nazi analogue for “honors exist without a racial state.” Use lightly so the Nazi case is not the only status example.

### 6 — Sterilization law

- **Use for:** The other half of the natalism: who may not reproduce.
- **Kind:** Primary.
- **Cite:** Gesetz zur Verhütung erbkranken Nachwuchses, 14 July 1933, RGBl. I S. 529.
- **Claim:** Compulsory sterilization of those labeled hereditarily ill (including “congenital feeble-mindedness,” schizophrenia, epilepsy, alcoholism). Estimates 300,000–400,000 sterilized by 1945. 1935 amendment allowed abortion in such cases. Meanwhile abortion and contraception were tightened for the “valuable.”
- **Caveat:** Already adjacent to chapter 5’s *Buck v. Bell* and China. Here the point is that the same regime that issued motherhood medals ran a sterilization mill. Do not rebuild the U.S. eugenics file.

### 7 — Did births rise?

- **Use for:** Magnitude, honestly.
- **Kind:** Secondary.
- **Cite:** The IÉSEG/working-paper literature using 1933, 1939, and 1970 censuses finds a transitory increase in fertility in the first Nazi years, partly marriage catch-up after the Depression. German TFR around 2.22 in 1933, 2.03 in 1939, 1.80 in 1944 (figures circulating in the German secondary literature; verify against a demographic table before locking).
- **Claim:** Period births rose from Depression lows. That is not a high-fertility people. Selection of *who* may reproduce is the durable fact.
- **Caveat:** War, then collapse. Do not credit the medals with replacement fertility.

## Analytical questions to keep on the page

Who chose? Who paid? Which lives counted? What was called a family? Can the apparent result be detached from violence or exclusion?
