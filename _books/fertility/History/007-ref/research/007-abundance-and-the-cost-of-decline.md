# Research: Abundance and the cost of decline

## What this section has to establish

Many extreme proposals are bad answers to real constraints. Abundance can make some coercion unnecessary — or make childless individualism easier. Decline is not costless. Land facing the two paths. No program.

## Suggested order of evidence

Housing as the binding material constraint (Dettling/Kearney, already ch. 6) → remote work and fertility (Davis et al. 2026, already ch. 6) → German pension credits / IFS care credits as recovered mechanisms → chapter 4 consequences as the cost of decline (point back) → UNFPA desired-vs-achieved (already chs. 1 and 6) → landing.

## Source cards

### 1 — House prices and births (already in chapter 6)

- **Use for:** A genuinely material constraint.
- **Cite:** Lisa J. Dettling and Melissa Schettini Kearney, “House Prices and Birth Rates,” *Journal of Public Economics* 110 (2014); NBER w17485.
- **Link:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w17485
- **Claim:** Rising prices reduced births among non-owners and raised them among owners. Same market, opposite short-run effects.
- **Already in the book?** Yes.

### 2 — Work from home and fertility (already in chapter 6)

- **Cite:** Steven J. Davis et al., “Work from Home and Fertility,” NBER w34963, 2026.
- **Link:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w34963
- **Claim:** Recent births and planned fertility higher among people WFH ≥1 day/week; both partners WFH associated with +0.32 estimated lifetime fertility vs neither. Not cleanly causal.
- **Already in the book?** Yes.
- **Use here:** Abundance of time-at-home as a recovered mechanism from “reorder work,” without a labor ban.

### 3 — Singapore childcare fee caps (catalog file)

- **Use for:** Cheap care as abundance — and as a policy that still prefers purchased care unless subsidies ignore working status (NDR 2026 will extend full subsidies regardless of applicant’s job).
- **Link:** https://www.population.gov.sg/marriage-parenthood-measures-at-national-day-rally-2026/

### 4 — German *Kindererziehungszeiten* (catalog file)

- **Use for:** Recovered mechanism from a childless tax: credit the years of raising children without a humiliation line-item.

### 5 — Chapter 4 consequences (already drafted)

- **Use for:** Decline is not costless — aging, fiscal state, 4-2-1 families, school closures, military recruitment, civilizational surplus.
- **Already in the book?** The whole of chapter 4. Point back. Do not rebuild empty classrooms.

### 6 — Compounding arithmetic (already in chapter 2)

- **Use for:** Irreversible generational contraction vs reversible policy.
- **Already in the book?** Chapter 2, compounding below replacement.

### 7 — UNFPA desired fertility (already in chapters 1 and 6)

- **Cite:** UNFPA, *State of World Population 2025: The Real Fertility Crisis*.
- **Link:** https://www.unfpa.org/swp2025
- **Claim:** Nearly 1 in 5 reproductive-age adults expected to miss desired family size; 31% of older respondents had fewer than ideal, 12% more. Money the most common barrier globally (39%), with huge national spread (58% Korea vs 19% Sweden).
- **Use here:** Passivity can be an injustice to people who already wanted children — still not a license for domination.

### 8 — Climate-worry and intended childlessness (already in chapter 6)

- **Cite:** Bastianelli et al., “Climate change worries and fertility intentions,” *Journal of Marriage and Family* 87, no. 2 (2025).
- **Already in the book?** Yes. Abundance/confidence vs pessimism.
