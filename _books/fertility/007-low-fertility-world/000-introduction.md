The people who will be thirty years old in 2056 are beginning to be born now. In many countries, there will be far fewer of them than the thirty-year-olds alive today. Even a remarkable fertility recovery next year cannot fill in the cohorts already missing. For the next few decades, much of the age structure is set.
{: .lede}

What happens after that is still open. A country can begin rebuilding generational balance. It can become very good at getting smaller. Or ultra-low fertility can persist until each generation has fewer people available to carry what the larger generations leave behind.

These are scenarios rather than forecasts. They begin from the same demographic inheritance and then separate around one question: does adaptation buy time for renewal, or gradually become the society's normal direction? The hopeful path comes first. Births recover, and almost nothing gets easier right away.
