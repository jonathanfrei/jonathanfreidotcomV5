Technology does not need to persuade anyone against children to reduce fertility. It only needs to occupy evenings, make solitude comfortable, and move attention elsewhere before intimacy deepens. The effect is difficult to isolate because phones and platforms arrived alongside economic and cultural changes. The mechanism is plausible but the evidence is incomplete.
{: .lede}

Online life maintains distant friendships, gives unusual people a way to find one another, and lets a person meet a future spouse outside their social circle. Thirty percent of American adults had used online dating by 2022. For rural residents or anyone with a thin local marriage market, that wider search is life-changing.

The same system converts courtship into a stream of suitors, and concentrates attention unevenly, encourages snap judgments, and promises a better option one swipe away. The platforms earn money from continued engagement; a successful marriage removes two customers. The incentives do not match the user's hope to leave.

## The evening that disappeared

Streaming video, pornography, and algorithmic feeds make a solitary night easy to fill. Digital substitutes are unusually abundant and frictionless and can satisfy enough of the desire for amusement and simulated company to postpone the harder work of knowing another person.

The U.S. Surgeon General's [2023 advisory on social connection](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/surgeon-general-social-connection-advisory.pdf) documented declines in several forms of in-person connection and treated loneliness and isolation as public-health problems. A lonely person may want a family intensely. At population scale, fewer friendships and congregations mean fewer routes through which partners meet and couples receive support.

Sex itself has declined among some young adults. Analysis of the General Social Survey found that in 2016–2018, [30.9 percent of men and 19.1 percent of women ages eighteen to twenty-four](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7293001/) reported no sexual activity in the previous year. Inactivity increased from 2000 to 2018 among young men and among men and women ages twenty-five to thirty-four. The increase was concentrated mainly among unmarried men and was associated with student status and, for men, weaker employment.

Pornography offers sexual novelty without courtship or obligation. It shapes expectations and reduces desire for a real partner for some users. It plainly separates sexual stimulation from the social path that once led toward marriage and children.

Digitally mediated companionship may deepen this separation. An artificial companion can be attentive and adjusted to its user. A human spouse cannot. If simulated intimacy becomes good enough to blunt loneliness without requiring compromise, some people will choose it. We do not yet know how many. The possibility deserves attention before a generation has spent its courtship years inside the experiment.

Technology serves the ends built into its design and the habits built by its users. It can help people find spouses and coordinate family life. It can also remove the useful friction through which patience and commitment develop. A civilization does not renew itself through connection counts. It needs people to meet in person, accept one another's limits, and remain.
