The [International Space Station has been continuously inhabited for twenty-five years](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/johnson/25-years-of-space-station-technology-driving-exploration/), and it remains an outpost. Its residents arrive as trained adults. Cargo vehicles bring provisions and replacement parts; mission control surrounds every crew with expertise on Earth. This is an extraordinary form of exploration. A settlement begins when absence from Earth becomes survivable.
{: .lede}

The distinction appears in the water. NASA recently demonstrated [98 percent water recovery aboard the station](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/iss-research/nasa-achieves-water-recovery-milestone-on-international-space-station/), close to its target for exploration beyond low Earth orbit. Two percent sounds small until it is repeated without resupply. The machinery also requires filters and maintenance. Closing a loop means keeping it closed after the specialist who designed it is gone.

## The first child changes the project

An expedition is organized around healthy adults selected for danger. A town must make room for children and human frailty. Human reproduction beyond Earth is largely unstudied. NASA's current research still relies heavily on animal and cellular experiments to understand how altered gravity affects development. Radiation outside Earth's protective magnetic field adds another concern.

This uncertainty requires careful biological work before anyone declares a base permanent. Shielding and artificial gravity may reduce the risks. Until children can be born and raised safely, an off-world population remains a rotating workforce supplied by Earth.

Children also require adults whose lives extend beyond the immediate mission. A teacher preserves attention for a lesson while an engineer worries about the oxygen plant. Parents sometimes choose the safer afternoon over the commander's efficient schedule. These ordinary divided loyalties are healthy. The founding objective should serve the people living there.

## How many is enough

Researchers have not agreed on a minimum population for a self-sustaining settlement. Genetic models produce very different answers because they assume different voyage lengths and reproductive rules. Cameron Smith's review of multigenerational interstellar travel proposed a founding population in the [rough range of 14,000 to 44,000](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actaastro.2013.12.013), much larger than older estimates of a few hundred. Other models have produced lower numbers under tight management. Every estimate remains far from a demonstrated plan for a real colony.

Genetic diversity is only the floor. A settlement can avoid inbreeding and fail when a crop disease defeats its food system. Resilience requires enough families that one accident does not erase a generation. It also needs enough social room for a person to leave a bad community without leaving the habitable world.

Governance will grow from practical control. Whoever allocates oxygen and return seats possesses unusual power. A company charter or mission command may work during construction, but children born under it did not sign a contract to become permanent crew. Durable law must recognize them as citizens and remain capable of correcting the founders.

A visitor looks back at Earth. A settler buries a parent and teaches a child the names of places neither of them intends to leave. The technology makes that scene possible. Family and law make it a civilization.
