The Guardian had a short piece <a href="http://www.theguardian.com/technology/2014/oct/12/happy-20th-anniversary-dave-winer-inventor-of-the-blog">celebrating the inventor of the blog</a>, Dave Winer.

<blockquote>Twenty years ago this week, a software developer in California ushered in a new era in how we communicate. His name is <a href="http://davewiner.com/">Dave Winer</a> and on 7 October 1994 he published his first <a href="http://scripting.com/davenet/">blog post</a>. He called it Davenet then, and he’s been writing it most days since then. In the process, he has become one of the internet’s elders, as eminent in his way as <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vint_Cerf">Vint Cerf</a>, <a href="http://groups.csail.mit.edu/ana/People/Clark.html">Dave Clark</a>,<a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doc_Searls">Doc Searls</a>, <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Lessig">Lawrence Lessig</a>, <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Weinberger">Dave Weinberger</a> or even <a href="http://www.w3.org/People/Berners-Lee/">Tim Berners-Lee</a>.</blockquote>

Naturally, Dave also had a personal reflection on pros and cons of his&nbsp;<a href="http://scripting.com/2014/10/07/20YearsOfBlogging.html">20 years of blogging</a>.

<blockquote>In 20 years of blogging and developing software for blogging, you meet a lot of people, and some of them <i>do</i> share love with you. To me that was always the wonder of blogging. I remember very clearly, in 1999 or 2000, looking at a blogroll and seeing dozens of names, mostly people I had never heard of, all of whom had blogs. It was at that moment that I realized that it had worked. But I was in for a rude shock when I clicked the links, they were all talking about me, and they didn’t like me! Oy.</blockquote>
