Today’s Daily *ism is another artistic (revolutionary) movement that came out of the modernist period:

> **Surrealism** is a cultural movement that began in the early 1920s, and is best known for the visual artworks and writings of the group members.
>
> Surrealist works feature the element of surprise, unexpected juxtapositions and [non sequitur](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non_sequitur_\(humor\) "Non sequitur \(humor\)"); however, many Surrealist artists and writers[_[who?](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Avoid_weasel_words "Wikipedia:Avoid weasel words")_] regard their work as an expression of the philosophical movement first and foremost, with the works being an artifact. Leader [André Breton](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Breton "André Breton") was explicit in his assertion that Surrealism was above all a revolutionary movement.
>
> Surrealism developed out of the [Dada](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dada "Dada") activities during [World War I](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I") and the most important center of the movement was [Paris](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris"). From the 1920s onward, the movement spread around the globe, eventually affecting the [visual arts](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_art "Visual art"), [literature](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literature "Literature"), [film](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film "Film") and [music](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music "Music") of many countries and languages, as well as [political](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics "Politics") thought and practice, [philosophy](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy "Philosophy") and [social theory](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_theory "Social theory").
