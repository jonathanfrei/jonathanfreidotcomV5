Here’s a political system that’s bringing down many parts of Europe. It’s also one that has to go by many other names, since its popularity is sagging, and its accomplishments leave such carnage in its wake.

> **Socialism** is an [economic system](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_system "Economic system") in which the [means of production](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Means_of_production "Means of production") are [publicly](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_ownership "Public ownership") or [commonly owned](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_ownership "Common ownership") and controlled co-operatively, or a [political philosophy](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_philosophy "Political philosophy") advocating such a system.[[1]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism#cite_note-0)[[2]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism#cite_note-1) As a form of social organization, socialism is based on co-operative social relations and [self-management](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-management "Self-management"); relatively equal power-relations and the reduction or elimination of hierarchy in the management of economic and political affairs.[[3]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism#cite_note-2)[[4]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism#cite_note-3)
>
> Socialist economies are based upon [production for use](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_for_use "Production for use") and the direct allocation of economic inputs to satisfy economic demands and human needs ([use value](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Use_value "Use value")); accounting is based on [physical quantities](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calculation_in_kind "Calculation in kind") of resources, some physical magnitude, or a direct measure of [labor-time](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time-based_currency "Time-based currency").[[5]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism#cite_note-4)[[6]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism#cite_note-5)Goods and services for consumption are distributed through markets, and distribution of income is based on the principle of [individual merit](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meritocracy "Meritocracy")/[individual contribution](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_each_according_to_his_contribution "To each according to his contribution").[[7]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism#cite_note-6)
>
> As a [political movement](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_movement "Political movement"), socialism includes a diverse array of political philosophies, ranging from [reformism](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformism "Reformism") to [revolutionary socialism](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_socialism "Revolutionary socialism").  _[State socialist](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_socialist "State socialist")_ currents of socialism advocate for the [nationalisation](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalisation "Nationalisation") of the means of production, distribution and exchange as a strategy for implementing socialism; while [social democrats](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_democracy "Social democracy") advocate [public control](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_interventionism "Economic interventionism") of capital within the framework of a [market economy](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_economy "Market economy"). [Libertarian socialists](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian_socialism "Libertarian socialism") and [anarchists](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism "Anarchism") reject using the [state](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_government "Central government") to build socialism, arguing that socialism will, and must, either arise spontaneously or be built from the bottom up utilizing the strategy of [dual power](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_power "Dual power"). They promote direct worker-ownership of the means of production alternatively through independent [syndicates](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syndicalism "Syndicalism"), [workplace democracies](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workplace_democracy "Workplace democracy"), or [worker cooperatives](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worker_cooperative "Worker cooperative").
>
> Modern socialism originated from an 18th-century intellectual and [working class](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_class "Working class") [political movement](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_movement "Political movement") that criticised the effects of [industrialisation](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialisation "Industrialisation")and [private property](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_property "Private property") on society. [Utopian socialists](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utopian_socialism "Utopian socialism") such as [Robert Owen](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Owen "Robert Owen") (1771–1858), tried to found self-sustaining communes by secession from a capitalist society. [Henri de Saint Simon](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_de_Saint_Simon "Henri de Saint Simon") (1760–1825), who coined the term  _socialisme_ , advocated [technocracy](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technocracy_\(bureaucratic\) "Technocracy \(bureaucratic\)") and industrial planning.[[8]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism#cite_note-fsmitha.com-7) Saint-Simon, [Friedrich Engels](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Engels "Friedrich Engels") and [Karl Marx](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Marx "Karl Marx") advocated the creation of a society that allows for the widespread application of modern technology to rationalise economic activity by eliminating the [anarchy](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchy "Anarchy") of capitalist production that results in instability and cyclical crises of [overproduction](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overproduction "Overproduction").[[9]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism#cite_note-8)[[10]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism#cite_note-9)
>
> Socialists inspired by the [Soviet model of economic development](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_the_Soviet_Union "Economy of the Soviet Union"), such as [Marxist-Leninists](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxist-Leninists "Marxist-Leninists"), have advocated the creation of centrally [planned economies](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planned_economy "Planned economy") directed by a [single-party state](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-party_state "Single-party state") that owns the means of production. Others, including [Yugoslavian](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia "Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia"), [Hungarian](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Republic_of_Hungary "People's Republic of Hungary"), [East German](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany "East Germany") and [Chinese](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Republic_of_China "People's Republic of China") [communist governments](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_state "Communist state") in the 1970s and 1980s, instituted various forms of [market socialism](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_socialism "Market socialism"),[_[citation needed](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_] combining co-operative and state ownership models with the free market [exchange](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade "Trade") and [free price system](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_price_system "Free price system") (but not free prices for the means of production).[[11]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism#cite_note-10)
