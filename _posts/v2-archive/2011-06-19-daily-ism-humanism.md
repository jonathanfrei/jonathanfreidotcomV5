Today, I’m going to bring up a school of philosophy.

> **Humanism** is an approach in [study](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observational_study "Observational study"), [philosophy](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy "Philosophy"), or practice that focuses on human [values](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Values "Values") and concerns. The term can mean [several things](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism_\(disambiguation\) "Humanism \(disambiguation\)"), for example:
>
>   1. [A cultural movement](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_humanism "Renaissance humanism") of the Italian Renaissance based on the study of classical works.
>   2. An approach to education that uses literary means or a focus on the [humanities](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanities "Humanities") to inform students.
>   3. A variety of perspectives in philosophy and [social science](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_science "Social science") which affirm some notion of ’[human nature](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_nature "Human nature")’ (by contrast with [anti-humanism](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-humanism "Anti-humanism")).
>   4. A [secular](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secular "Secular") ideology which espouses [reason](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reason "Reason"), [ethics](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethics "Ethics"), and [justice](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice "Justice"), whilst specifically rejecting [supernatural](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supernatural "Supernatural") and [religious](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious "Religious") [dogma](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogma "Dogma") as a basis of[morality](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morality "Morality") and decision-making.
>

>
> The last interpretation may be attributed to [Secular Humanism](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secular_humanism "Secular humanism") as a specific humanistic life stance.[[1]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism#cite_note-0) Modern meanings of the word have therefore come to be associated with a rejection of appeals to the [supernatural](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supernatural "Supernatural") or to some higher authority.[[2]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism#cite_note-1)[[3]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism#cite_note-2) This interpretation may be directly contrasted with other prominent uses of the term in traditional religious circles.[[4]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism#cite_note-3) Humanism of this strand arose from a trajectory extending from the [deism](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deism "Deism") and[anti-clericalism](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-clericalism "Anti-clericalism") of the [Enlightenment](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment "Age of Enlightenment"), the various [secular](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secular "Secular") movements of the 19th century (such as [positivism](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positivism "Positivism")), and the overarching expansion of the scientific project.
>
> **Humanist** , **humanism** , and **humanistic** may also refer simply and loosely to  _literary culture_.[[5](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism#cite_note-4)
