Here I’m taking us back to an outdated political system. I’m not aware of any active political movements trying to bring it back.

> **Manorialism,** an essential element of [feudal](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feudalism "Feudalism") society,[[1]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manorialism#cite_note-0) was the organizing principle of rural economy that originated in the [villa system](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_villa "Roman villa") of the Late Roman Empire,[[2]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manorialism#cite_note-1) was widely practiced in [medieval](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages "Middle Ages") western and parts of central [Europe](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe "Europe"), and was slowly replaced by the advent of a money-based [market economy](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_economy "Market economy") and new forms of agrarian contract.
>
> Manorialism was characterised by the vesting of legal and economic power in a lord, supported economically from his own direct landholding and from the obligatory contributions of a legally subject part of the peasant population under his jurisdiction. These obligations could be payable in several ways, in labor (the French term  _[corvée](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corv%C3%A9e "Corvée")_ is conventionally applied), [in kind](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payment_in_kind "Payment in kind"), or, on rare occasions, in [coin](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coin "Coin").
>
> In examining the origins of the monastic [cloister](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloister "Cloister"), Walter Horn found that “as a manorial entity the [Carolingian](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian "Carolingian") [monastery](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monastery "Monastery")… differed little from the fabric of a feudal estate, save that the corporate community of men for whose sustenance this organization was maintained consisted of monks who served God in chant and spent much of their time in reading and writing.”[[3]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manorialism#cite_note-2)
>
> Manorialism died slowly and piecemeal, along with its most vivid feature in the landscape, the [open field system](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_field_system "Open field system"). It outlasted [serfdom](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serfdom "Serfdom")as it outlasted feudalism: “primarily an economic organization, it could maintain a warrior, but it could equally well maintain a[capitalist](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalist "Capitalist") landlord. It could be self-sufficient, yield produce for the market, or it could yield a money rent.”[[4]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manorialism#cite_note-3) The last feudal dues in France were abolished at the [French Revolution](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution "French Revolution"). In parts of eastern Germany, the  _Rittergut_ manors of [Junkers](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junker "Junker") remained until [World War II](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II").[[5]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manorialism#cite_note-4)
